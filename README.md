# Mathematikz

An interactive website for learning mathematics, from the ground up and free. It is the maths branch of the Ikz family, a sibling of Cirkitz and Mekanikz (same layout and dark theme, with a violet accent).

The course has eleven units so far: **the unit circle**, **factoring and rational expressions**, **equations and inequalities**, **exponents, radicals and logarithms**, **functions and graphs**, **trigonometry, continued** **sequences, series and the binomial theorem** **complex numbers and vectors** **limits and derivatives** **integration** and **series and advanced calculus**, and is meant to grow from algebra up to advanced calculus (the full course map is in `PLAN.md`).

- **Learn**: thirty-seven ordered lessons in seven units, each with worked examples, check questions, progress tracking and interactive figures.

  *Unit 1, the unit circle:*
  1. *Degrees and radians*: what a radian is, converting both ways, the sixteen common angles, negative and coterminal angles, arc length.
  2. *The unit circle*: sin and cos as coordinates, where the exact values come from (the 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 triangles), quadrants, reference angles, ASTC.
  3. *Exact values of sin, cos and tan*: the four-step method, tan undefined, rationalising, combining surds.
  4. *Solving trig equations*: equations such as `2cos(x) − √3 = 0` on `0 ≤ x ≤ 2π` (or in degrees), the axis cases, squares and factors.
  5. *Inverse trig functions*: principal values of arcsin, arccos and arctan at unit-circle values, and compositions like `arccos(cos θ)` and `sin(arccos x)`.

  *Unit 2, factoring and rational expressions:*
  1. *Expanding and special products*: the distributive law, two and three brackets, squares, cubes, several letters.
  2. *Factoring polynomials*: common factors, trinomials (including leading coefficient not 1), squares and cubes, grouping, substitution, several letters.
  3. *Polynomials and their roots*: degree, the remainder and factor theorems, long division, solving cubics and quartics, the rational root theorem.
  4. *Simplifying rational expressions*: factor and cancel, restrictions, holes and asymptotes, opposite factors, cubes, several letters.
  5. *Operations on rational expressions*: multiplying, dividing, adding and subtracting with factored LCDs, complex fractions, difference quotients.
  6. *Rational equations and solving for a letter*: clearing fractions, extraneous solutions, rearranging formulas.
  *Unit 3, equations and inequalities:*
  1. *Linear equations and rearranging formulas*: the balance method, brackets, fractions, no solution and identities, making a letter the subject.
  2. *Solving quadratic equations*: the zero product rule, square roots, a leading coefficient other than 1, completing the square, hidden quadratics.
  3. *The quadratic formula and the discriminant*: counting roots in advance, the vertex, sum and product of roots, solving for a parameter.
  4. *Inequalities and sign charts*: interval notation, flipping the sign, compound, quadratic and rational inequalities.
  5. *Absolute value equations and inequalities*: distance, the three cases, extraneous answers, sums of distances.
  6. *Systems of equations*: substitution, elimination, special cases, word problems, three unknowns, a line meeting a parabola.
  *Unit 4, exponents, radicals and logarithms:*
  1. *Exponents and the laws of powers*: the eight laws, zero, negative and fractional exponents, simplifying to positive exponents, scientific notation, equations with the unknown in the exponent.
  2. *Radicals and surds*: simplest form, like radicals, multiplying and expanding, rationalising (including with a conjugate), radical equations and extraneous roots.
  3. *Logarithms and exponential equations*: log as inverse of exponent, the laws, change of base, solving exponential and logarithmic equations with a domain check, compound growth and half-life.
  *Unit 5, functions and graphs:*
  1. *Functions, notation and domain*: evaluating, domain and range, piecewise functions, continuity, the difference quotient.
  2. *Transformations of graphs*: the six basic moves, how a point moves, order of transformations, parabolas from the vertex.
  3. *Composition and inverse functions*: f(g(x)), the horizontal line test, finding inverses, the reflection in y = x, restricted domains.
  4. *Graphs of polynomials*: end behaviour, multiplicity, turning points, sign diagrams, building an equation from roots.
  5. *Rational functions and asymptotes*: vertical and horizontal and oblique asymptotes, holes, intercepts.
  6. *Modelling with functions*: average rate of change, linear, quadratic and piecewise models, even and odd functions, where a function rises or falls.
  *Unit 6, trigonometry, continued:*
  1. *Right-angled triangles*: SOH CAH TOA, finding sides and angles, the exact special triangles, elevation, depression and three dimensions.
  2. *The sine and cosine rules*: choosing the rule, area, the ambiguous case, bearings.
  3. *Reciprocal functions and identities*: sec, csc, cot, the Pythagorean identities, simplifying, proving, and refuting a false identity.
  4. *Sum, difference and double angle formulas*: exact values at 15° and 75°, half angles, collapsing expressions, R sin(x + α).
  5. *Graphs of trigonometric functions*: amplitude, period, phase shift, midline, equations from graphs, asymptotes, periodic models.
  6. *Solving trigonometric equations in general*: general solutions, multiple and shifted angles, quadratics in sin x, and equations that need an identity.
  *Unit 7, sequences, series and the binomial theorem:*
  1. *Sequences and recurrence relations*: arithmetic and geometric sequences, finding terms, two given terms, recurrences.
  2. *Arithmetic and geometric series*: the sum formulas, how many terms, sum to infinity, recurring decimals, a bouncing ball.
  3. *Sigma notation and proof by induction*: standard sums, closed forms, telescoping, and induction on sums and divisibility.
  4. *The binomial theorem*: nCr and Pascal's triangle, expanding, a single term or coefficient, approximating a power.
  5. *Limits of sequences*: convergence, rational limits, rⁿ, the squeeze, and recurrences as fixed points (with a cobweb diagram).
  *Unit 8, complex numbers and vectors:*
  1. *Complex numbers*: i, a + bi arithmetic, powers of i, conjugates and division, equating parts, quadratics with complex roots (with an Argand diagram).
  2. *Polar form, de Moivre and roots*: modulus and argument, multiplying by adding angles, de Moivre, nth roots (with a roots-of-a-number polygon), Euler's formula.
  3. *Vectors in two and three dimensions*: components, magnitude, unit vectors, displacements and dividing a segment, parallel vectors.
  4. *The dot and cross products*: angles, perpendicularity, projections, cross products and areas (with a 2D dot-product lab and a 3D cross-product lab).
  5. *Lines and planes in space*: vector equations, where lines meet, planes through three points, distances and angles.
  *Unit 9, limits and derivatives:*
  1. *Limits and continuity*: one-sided limits, substitution, factoring and rationalising, limits at infinity, sin x / x, continuity and the types of discontinuity (with an approach-the-limit figure).
  2. *The derivative from first principles*: secant to tangent (with a secant figure), the limit definition, tangent and normal lines, velocity, differentiability.
  3. *Rules of differentiation*: power, chain, product and quotient rules and the standard derivatives (with a function-and-derivative figure).
  4. *Implicit differentiation and higher derivatives*: implicit and parametric differentiation, second derivatives, inverse functions, inverse trig and logarithmic differentiation (with a circle-tangent figure).
  5. *Applications of the derivative*: stationary points, concavity, optimisation (fence, open box), related rates (sliding ladder), linear approximation, Newton's method and l'Hôpital's rule.
  *Unit 10, integration:*
  1. *Antiderivatives and indefinite integrals*: reversing differentiation, + C, the standard integrals, initial value problems and motion (with a family-of-curves figure).
  2. *The definite integral and the fundamental theorem*: Riemann sums, the theorem in both parts, properties, area between curves, displacement versus distance (figures for sums, accumulated area and area between curves).
  3. *Integration by substitution*: linear substitutions, f(g(x))g′(x), changing limits, rewriting x, odd trig powers (with a step-by-step guide).
  4. *Integration by parts, partial fractions and trigonometric integrals*: simplify first, LIATE, definite parts, splitting fractions, sin² and cos² (with a step-by-step guide).
  5. *Applications of integration*: average value, accumulated change, discs, washers and shells, Simpson's rule, improper integrals (with a disc-slicing figure).
  *Unit 11, series and advanced calculus:*
  1. *Infinite series and convergence tests*: partial sums, geometric and telescoping series, divergence, p-series, comparison, ratio and alternating tests, absolute versus conditional convergence (with a partial-sums figure).
  2. *Power series*: radius and interval of convergence with endpoint tests, differentiating and integrating term by term (with an interval-of-convergence figure).
  3. *Taylor and Maclaurin series*: the standard series, Taylor polynomials about a point, error bounds, limits and integrals by series (with a polynomial-approximation figure).
  4. *Functions of several variables and partial derivatives*: partials, gradient, directional derivative, tangent plane, chain rule, second derivative test, Lagrange multipliers (with a contour-map figure).
  5. *Double and triple integrals*: iterated integrals, general regions, reversing the order, polar coordinates, the Gaussian integral, triple integrals (with a grid-sum figure).
- **Practice**: *Free practice* generates endless questions with worked solutions by unit, lesson and difficulty. *Section practice* gives a repeatable 10-question round for every lesson and the whole unit (new numbers each time, concept questions mixed in), a review of what you missed, and your best score. Answers are typed and checked by value, so `√3/2`, `sqrt(3)/2` and `1/√2` all count, and `5π/6` can be typed `5pi/6`. Decimals are rejected where an exact answer is wanted. In Unit 2 you type algebra (`(x-1)(x+1)^2`, `x^2 - 3x`, `(x+2)/(x+3)`) and it is checked by value: an expansion must have no brackets left, a factorisation must be *complete* (no bracket that can still be factored, no common factor left inside), and a fraction must be in lowest terms. The feedback says which of these is missing. In Unit 3 you give exact roots (`2+sqrt(3), 2-sqrt(3)` or `2 ± sqrt(3)`), ordered pairs, and solution sets written as inequalities (`x < -2 or x >= 3`) or in interval notation (`(-inf, -2) U [3, inf)`); the set is compared exactly, open and closed ends included.
In Unit 4, a radical answer must be in *simplest form* (no square or cube factor left under the root, no root in a denominator), answers with exponents must use positive exponents, and decimal answers to exponential equations can be typed as a number or as the working, for example `ln(20)/ln(3)`.
- **Games**: Sprint (60 seconds, streak bonus), Survival (three lives, rising difficulty), Match-up (memory pairs from twenty-eight decks, for example inequalities and interval notation, simplifying roots, functions and their inverses, trig identities and the exact values at 15° and 75°) and Root hunter (click where a graph crosses the axis). All use the same generators as Practice and keep personal bests on the device.
- **Explore**: a large draggable unit circle with exact readouts, a table of every special angle (click a row), and a degree/radian converter.

## Develop

```bash
npm install
npm run dev     # http://localhost:5175
npm test        # exact-value engine, answer parser, question generators, course content
npm run build   # type-check and production build into dist/
```

Pushing to `main` deploys to GitHub Pages via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`.

## How it works

- `src/math/exact.ts` is the engine: surds as `(p/q)√r`, angles as multiples of π, exact sin/cos/tan at every multiple of 30° and 45°, reference angles, equation solving over `[0, 2π]` and principal values of the inverse functions. Lesson tables are computed from it, so lessons and quizzes cannot disagree.
- `src/math/parse.ts` reads what a student types (`5π/6`, `sqrt(3)/2`, `-1/2`, `150°`, lists and pairs).
- `src/math/algebra.ts` is the algebra engine: polynomials in several variables with rational coefficients, rational expressions, exact division, rational roots, equation solving, a forgiving expression parser (`2x(x+1)`, `x²`, `(x+y)^3`) and the answer checker for expanded, factored, fractional and solve-for answers.
- `src/math/solve.ts` is the Unit 3 engine: exact quadratic roots, solution sets as lists of intervals (with a parser for typed inequalities and interval notation), sign charts, and linear systems.
- `src/math/equationQuestions.ts` has the Unit 3 generators (about 40). Every question also carries the equation or inequality as a predicate, and the tests check the answer against it on a dense grid of values, independently of how the answer was built.
- `src/math/radicals.ts` is the Unit 4 radical engine (extracting roots, exact arithmetic with a + b√r) and the simplest-form check on what a student types. `src/math/powerQuestions.ts` has the Unit 4 generators (about 25), tested the same way as Unit 3's: answers are plugged back into the equation as written.
- `src/math/functionQuestions.ts` has the Unit 5 generators (about 45): domains and ranges as solution sets, transformations done by substitution into the formula, inverses and compositions checked symbolically, polynomial and rational graph features, and one-word answers (even, odd, up, down, crosses, touches).
- `src/math/trigAlgebra.ts` checks trigonometric expressions: an answer must be equal to the model answer at nine unrelated angles, not be the question written back, and use no more trig functions than the model answer (and sometimes must contain a given form such as 2x). `src/math/trigQuestions.ts` has the Unit 6 generators (about 45): triangles built from their true measurements, identities from verified templates, and trigonometric equations whose hand-derived solution lists are checked by a numeric root scanner before they are ever shown.
- `src/math/calculus.ts` checks answers that are formulas in x, including antiderivatives (accepted up to + C, by checking that the difference from the model answer is a constant) (a derivative is accepted when it agrees with the model answer at many sample points, in any equal form) and has the numerical derivative and limit helpers that the tests use. `calcLimits.ts`, `calcRules.ts` and `calcApps.ts` hold the Unit 9 generators (about 60); every derivative is checked against a numerical derivative inside the generator, and every limit against a numerical estimate, so a wrong answer cannot reach a student.
- `intAnti.ts`, `intTech.ts` and `intQuestions.ts` hold the Unit 10 generators (about 45): every antiderivative is verified by differentiating it and every definite integral by Simpson's rule inside the generator.
- `serQuestions.ts`, `mvQuestions.ts` and `advQuestions.ts` hold the Unit 11 generators (about 45): convergence verdicts are checked from partial sums or term ratios, Maclaurin polynomials against the function, partial derivatives against numerical partials (formulas in x and y are checked at many sample points), and double integrals against nested Simpson's rule.
- `src/math/complex.ts` is the exact complex-number engine (rational real and imaginary parts), and `src/math/cxQuestions.ts` has the Unit 8 generators (about 40), whose answers are checked as complex numbers, exact vectors or surds.
- `src/math/seqQuestions.ts` has the Unit 7 generators (about 35). Each closed form (an nth term, a sum, a binomial coefficient, a limit of a recurrence) is computed twice, by formula and by brute force, and the generator throws if they disagree, so a wrong formula cannot reach a student.
- `src/math/games.ts` is the logic of the games (decks, scoring, Root hunter rounds).
- `src/math/algebraQuestions.ts` has the Unit 2 generators (about 60). Each is built from explicit factors or roots, so the answer is known by construction, then run through the engine so the shown text cannot disagree with it.
- `src/math/questions.ts` has the question generators (about 30, in three levels, each with a worked solution) and the answer checker. Tests run every generator over many random seeds and confirm that each question accepts its own answer and rejects wrong ones.
- `src/learn/content/` holds the course as typed data. `src/learn/widgets/` has the interactive figures.

Limitations: exact values cover multiples of 30° and 45° only (not 15° or 75°). Equations are solved on `[0, 2π]` / `[0°, 360°]`, with general solutions only mentioned in a note. Only sin, cos and tan so far (no sec, csc, cot). In Units 2 and 3, factoring is over the rationals, quadratics with a negative discriminant have no solution (complex numbers come in Unit 8), and inequalities are over the reals. In Unit 2, factoring is over the rationals (no irrational or complex factors), solution sets are limited to rational roots, and the checker does not mark intermediate working, only the final answer.
